Every round, I read one open-source codebase cover to cover: the server, the clients, the lint rules, the CI, the PR templates, even the bot configs. Then I write up eight things it does better than most projects. Every repo I pick is one I'd hold up as an example of good engineering.

How each lesson works

  1. In short: the problem and the idea, in two sentences, at the top.
  2. The engineering: how the repo actually does it, with real code, numbers and quotes from its docs. Some lessons have a live demo you can poke at.
  3. Steal this: the idea in one paragraph you can take to your own code.
  4. Use it in your code: a prompt you paste into your coding agent. It checks whether the idea fits your repo before it changes anything.
  5. Read the source: links to the exact files the lesson is based on, so you can check my work.

Each codebase ends with power-user tips for actually using the tool.
