Popular open-source projects now get more issues and PRs than any team can read, and a growing share are written by agents that nobody reviewed. This repo's answer is the bluntest one I've seen: everything from a new contributor is closed on arrival.

How the gate works

  1. A new contributor opens an issue or PR. A workflow closes it with a comment explaining why.
  2. Maintainers read the closed issues daily and reopen the good ones.
  3. To approve someone, a maintainer replies with one word. lgtmi means your future issues stay open; lgtm also lets you open PRs.
  4. A workflow parses that comment, checks the commenter has write access, and commits your name to a plain-text file, .github/APPROVED_CONTRIBUTORS. It has 270 people in it.

One detail I like: the gate reads that file from the default branch, never from the PR. A PR that adds its own author to the list doesn't approve itself.

A short quality bar

You must understand your code. If you cannot explain what your changes do and how they interact with the rest of the system, your PR will be closed.

  • Using AI to write code is fine. "Submitting AI-generated slop without understanding it is not."
  • An issue must fit on one screen and be written in your own voice. If you use an LLM, post its text as a clearly labeled follow-up.
  • Issues opened Friday to Sunday may wait until the Monday review.
  • Ignore the guide twice, or flood the tracker through automation, and you're blocked.

Where AI fits

The FAQ answers the obvious question, "why not let AI triage everything?":

AI can help group duplicates, summarize reports, and spot missing information. It is not trusted to make final maintainer decisions.

And the other obvious question, "is this hostile to contributors?": "No. It is a guardrail against burnout and tracker spam."

Compared with round 1

Round 1's repo also wanted maintainer approval before a PR, but asked for it as a link inside the PR template. This one enforces it with a bot, before any human spends time reading.

Steal this

When volume outruns reviewers, make "closed until a human says yes" the default. Keep the approval list in the repo, read it from the main branch, and make approval a one-word comment so saying yes costs nothing.
